Precisely—it comes down to privileges and actions, not changing policies.

Once an attacker elevates their privileges to Administrator/SYSTEM, the operating system gives them native permission to run commands and create accounts. They don't need to rewrite system policies to permit account creation because Administrators are already permitted to do that out of the box.

Account Creation (Action): The attacker uses existing administrative privileges to execute an standard system action (net user hacker /add).

Policy (Rulebook): Changing a policy means altering system-wide rules (e.g., "Must passwords be 12 characters long?" or "Should the firewall block Port 80?").

Unless a system rule specifically blocks them from an action, attackers have no reason to modify policy settings—doing so creates unnecessary noise and increases their chances of being caught.
