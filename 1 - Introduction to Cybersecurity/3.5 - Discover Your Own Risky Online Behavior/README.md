# ![1-up] 3.5 - Discover Your Own Risky Online Behavior

## Objectives

Analyze a few fictional scenarios regarding common cybersecurity risks that users may find in their day-to-day lives and learn the recommended security measures for each.

## Methodology

1. Analyzed scenario 1, which had examples of social media posts. Some of them had personal information such as addresses, bank accounts and valuable goods.
2. Analyzed scenario 2, where a user logged into many different online services with different passwords and considered writing them down.
3. Analyzed scenario 3, where a user connected to a public Wi-Fi hotspot that seemed like a good connection option but was actually hosted by a threat actor.
4. Analyzed scenario 4, where a user downloaded a trial version of a program from an untrusted source. They then proceeded to receive many notifications warning that there had been security breaches to many of their accounts.
5. Analyzed scenario 5, where a user opened a website and saw a scary warning saying that their computer was infected. There was also a call to action telling them to download a specific antivirus program to fix the issue.
6. Analyzed scenario 6, where an employee received an email that claimed to be from the IT department, however, the email sender was someone from outside the organization and there were grammar mistakes in the subject and body.

## Key takeaway

All of the examples presented are very common situations that can happen to any of us. Fortunately, there are available and easy-to-use security measures for each of them:

* When posting anything on social media, any personal/sensitive information could make hackers have you as a target of tactics such as phishing emails or phone call scams. If the information is specific enough, they could even directly access your accounts and compromise your data and finances. That is why it's always important to ask yourself: can the information in this post harm me in any way? Considering that any person online can see this, is this information something that I would be willing to show to total strangers?
* Remembering passwords can quickly become a headache considering how many online services we have to access every day. That is why password managers are recommended, which save them for all of your accounts in encrypted form. This makes it easy to access any online accounts safely and dismisses the need to write down private information. These tools also help generate complex passwords, enhancing security even further.
* Connecting to public Wi-Fi hotspots is always a risk, because you never know who administers them or what security measures are being put into practice (if any). To make sure your data won't be intercepted by hackers, VPNs are highly recommended. They provide a safe encrypted connection tunnel to the hotspot, so that any captured traffic cannot be interpreted.
* Downloading programs from untrusted sources is almost certain to expose your device to malware, which could then affect your legitimate software, hardware components or data. Even if it may seem like an easy opportunity (such as a free program), you should always download any programs from official websites.
* It is very common to stumble upon scary pop-ups while browsing the web. If such a situation happens, you must never follow the instructions in the window: simply close it and, to be sure, run an antivirus program. Following or downloading anything could compromise your device with malware.
* Phishing e-mails are unfortunately very common, aiming to trick unsuspecting users into clicking links to fake websites or downloading malicious attachments. It is always important to verify the sender address, grammar, link structure and language being used, as even minor changes in any of these could prove to be warning signs.
